# Cold Outreach Strategy — Custom AI Automation Systems

> **Niche focus:** Custom full-stack apps + AI automations (quote-automation & content-ops style builds).
> Custom frontend + n8n / core backend + AI agents + RAG chatbots (code & no-code).
> *(GoHighLevel niche is parked for later — separate playbook.)*

---

## 0. The core problem to solve first

My two proof builds live in **different industries**:

- **Build A** → industrial parts **quote management** (B2B distributor ops)
- **Build B** → finance/payroll **newsletter pipeline** (content ops)

For a portfolio this is fine. For **cold outreach it's a problem**, because a pitch like *"I build custom AI systems"* sounds like everyone and matches no specific pain → no replies.

**The fix:** Don't pick an *industry*. Pick a repeatable **system type ("wedge")** to lead with. I can still take any custom project, but outreach hammers ONE use case so prospects self-identify.

---

## 1. Pick the spearhead wedge

Repeatable systems my portfolio already proves I can build:

| Wedge | What it does | Who has this pain | Repeatable |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AI quote / RFQ automation** (quote-mgmt build) | Reads inbound emails/RFQs → extracts parts → finds suppliers/prices → generates quote | Distributors, wholesalers, manufacturers, B2B suppliers | ★★★ |
| **AI email / document data extraction** | Inbound emails, PDFs, orders, invoices → structured data into their system | Anyone processing docs/orders by hand | ★★★ |
| **RAG chatbot on internal data** | Chatbot trained on their docs/SOPs/catalog for staff or customers | Support teams, SaaS, prof. services, ops-heavy SMBs | ★★★ |
| **AI content / newsletter ops** (newsletter build) | Scrape → filter → summarize → publish/send | Media, newsletters, agencies, niche B2B publishers | ★★ |
| **Custom ops dashboard + automation** | Replace spreadsheets/manual workflow with a real internal tool + automations | SMBs drowning in manual back-office work | ★★ |

### ✅ Recommendation
**Lead with "AI quote/RFQ automation" or "AI email/document processing."**

Why:
- Pain is **measurable in hours and money** ("4 hours/day re-typing emails").
- The buyer is **obvious**.
- ~70% of the quote-mgmt build is **reusable** for every new client → real margins, not one-off freelancing.

## 2. Define the buyer (ICP)

For the quote/document wedge, the ideal customer:

- **Company size:** 10–200 employees (enough volume; founder/ops lead buys fast, no procurement committee).
- **Industry:** B2B processing lots of inbound requests — distributors, wholesalers, manufacturers, logistics, suppliers, specialty trades.
- **Buyer title:** **Operations Manager, COO, Founder/Owner, Head of Sales Ops.** NOT the IT department.
- **Trigger:** scaling, drowning in manual work, or hiring people to do data entry.

**One-sentence ICP (the filter for every list I build):**
> "B2B distributors/suppliers, 10–200 staff, whose ops or sales team manually processes inbound quote requests / emails."

---

## 3. Productize a low-risk entry offer

Custom builds scare people ("how much? how long? what if it fails?"). Sell a **small fixed first step**, then the big build:

1. **Free "Automation Audit"** — 30-min call, map their manual workflow, show what's automatable. *(This is the sales mechanism.)*
2. **Paid Pilot / Sprint** — **$2k–$5k, 1–2 weeks** — build ONE working piece (e.g. email extraction). Low risk, proves it works.
3. **Full system** — **$8k–$40k+ build** + **monthly retainer $500–$3k/mo** (hosting, AI usage, maintenance, improvements).

> The **audit → pilot → full-build ladder** is what makes high-ticket custom work sellable cold. Nobody buys a $30k build from an email — they buy a $3k pilot, and the pilot sells the build.

## 4. Lead sourcing — from zero onwards

This is **NOT** an Apollo-blast game. Best leads come from **buying signals** — companies showing the manual pain *right now*. Ranked by quality:

### Tier 1 — Intent signals (highest reply rate)
- **Job boards.** Companies hiring for *"Data Entry Clerk," "Quote Coordinator," "Operations Assistant," "Order Processor," "Back Office Admin"* are telling you they're about to pay a salary for work I can automate. Search **LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, Glassdoor**. → **#1 source.**
- **Upwork / freelance boards.** Posts for "n8n automation," "Make.com," "Zapier," "AI chatbot," "scrape and process emails." Budget + exact problem. Mine where I came from.
- **Reddit / forums / communities:** r/automation, r/nocode, r/n8n, n8n community forum, Make community, indie hacker/SaaS communities. Each "how do I automate X" post is a lead.

### Tier 2 — Targeted lists (build with filters)
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** — industry (wholesale/manufacturing/logistics) + title (Ops Manager, COO, Founder) + headcount (11–200). Build 200–500.
- **Apollo.io** — same filters + verified emails + keyword filters for the vertical.
- **Industry directories & trade associations** — member directories. Niche but high-fit.

### Tier 3 — Inbound / authority (slower, compounding)
- **Post case studies on LinkedIn** — "Cut a distributor's quoting from 4 hrs → 10 min" + a Loom of the quote-automation system. This niche buys from people who *show* working systems.
- **Write the build up** as a short case-study PDF/page → lead magnet.

> **Start from literal zero this week:** LinkedIn Jobs + Indeed → search the data-entry / quote / ops-coordinator titles → build a list of 50 companies actively hiring for manual work. Warmest possible cold list.

## 5. Outreach messaging (signal-based, not generic)

### A) Company hiring a data-entry / quote role
> **Subject:** saw you're hiring a {Quote Coordinator}
>
> Hey {first_name} — noticed you're hiring for {role}. Quick thought before you do:
>
> A lot of that work — reading inbound requests, pulling the details, getting them into your system — can run automatically with an AI system. I recently built one for a B2B supplier that turned a 4-hour daily process into ~10 minutes.
>
> Not saying don't hire — but it might be worth seeing what's automatable first. Open to a 15-min look? I'll map it for free.

### B) Targeted ops lead (no specific signal)
> **Subject:** the manual part of {their workflow}
>
> Hey {first_name} — how does your team handle inbound {quote requests / orders / emails} today? Mostly manual?
>
> I build AI systems that read those, extract the details, and push them straight into your tools — one client cut a 4-hr/day task to minutes. Worth a quick look at yours?

### Rules for this niche
- **Reference the manual task by name**, not "AI." They feel the pain in the *task*.
- **Lead with the quote-automation result** (hours/time saved) — strongest proof.
- **CTA = the free audit** — low friction; that's where I close.
- **Email + LinkedIn combo** — ops leaders live on LinkedIn. Connect → soft DM → email follow-up.

## 6. Sending infrastructure (same base as prior playbook)

- Never send from main domain. Buy **2–3 secondary domains** (~$10/yr).
- **Google Workspace inboxes** ($6/mo each); start with 1–2 for this low-volume niche.
- Set **SPF + DKIM + DMARC** on every domain (non-negotiable).
- **Warm up 2–3 weeks** before real sends (Instantly / Smartlead).
- **~20–30 emails/inbox/day** max. Verify emails (Apollo / MillionVerifier), keep bounce < 3%.
- **Compliance:** B2B cold email legal in US (CAN-SPAM) — identify yourself, easy opt-out, physical address. EU/UK = stricter (GDPR); keep relevant, honor opt-outs.

---

## 7. Volume & expectations (high-ticket, low-volume)

- **High-touch, personalized** — not spray-and-pray.
- ~**30–50 personalized touches/day** (email + LinkedIn).
- Reply rate **5–15%** (signal-targeted).
- ~200–400 quality touches → handful of audit calls → 1–2 pilots → builds.
- **One client = $10k–$40k+** → need very few of them.
